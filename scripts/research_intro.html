Robotic manipulation requires more than choosing the right goal or executing an isolated skill. Over a long horizon, a robot must remember task state, act under physical constraints, and use execution feedback to decide what comes next. Final success alone offers limited insight into where this process breaks down.

Mind2Act World is a simulation benchmark for studying task reasoning, physical execution, and their coordination. Its three complementary views separate these demands: Mind evaluates maintaining task information and selecting the required operations; Act evaluates timely, precise, and stable execution under geometric, contact, and dynamic constraints. Mind2Act brings them together: task intent guides physically valid actions, while execution outcomes update remaining goals, action timing, and phase progression. This makes coordination an ongoing interaction throughout a task.

The benchmark comprises 15 tasks, each with Easy, Medium, and Hard configurations that vary memory load, planning dependencies, precision requirements, and timing pressure. Evaluation combines task success with stage progress and event-level evidence, helping distinguish errors in goal selection, physical execution, and the use of feedback. Shared observation and action interfaces, explicit timing conditions, and disclosure of execution assistance support comparisons across VLA/WAM policies, coding agents, and hybrid systems. Together, these views connect focused capabilities to sustained task completion and make failures easier to investigate.
